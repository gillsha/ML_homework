From Ratings to Recommendations:
A Mean-Only Outcome of Matrix Factorization

Elizaveta Gill · eagill@edu.hse.ru
HSE · Machine Learning and Recommender Systems · Homework A05 / Week 5
16 October 2026

Abstract

Explicit-rating recommendation requires learning from observed feedback while evaluating unseen user–movie pairs without leakage. This study implements 16-factor matrix factorization in TensorFlow.js on MovieLens 100K, using chronological per-user splits and a training-mean baseline. Both the instructor-default regularization of 0.05 and a controlled reduction to 0.005 produced effectively mean-only predictions, with independently verified test RMSE 1.2257193843 and macro Recall@10 0.02113749. The result illustrates why passing implementation checks and obtaining nonzero retrieval recall do not, by themselves, demonstrate learned personalization.

Index terms—matrix factorization, MovieLens, regularization, chronological evaluation, reproducibility.

1. Introduction

The task is to predict ratings and recommend ten movies not rated in a user's training or validation history. Sparse feedback is missing information, not a zero-valued preference. For example, a recommendation of Fargo to user 1 can recover a held-out positive, but a tied score cannot establish that the model learned to prefer that film.

The contributions are a functioning browser implementation with explicit evaluation eligibility and checkpoint restoration, and an evidence-based comparison that preserves an unsuccessful result rather than presenting numerical noise as an improvement. The business question is whether the lists reflect familiar tastes or support new discoveries.

2. Related Work

MovieLens and its documented collection context [1] provide explicit ratings rather than complete viewing or exposure records. The instructor's pinned HW5 specification [2] defines the factor model, chronological protocol, and retrieval task used here. TensorFlow.js [3] supplies tensor computation and automatic differentiation in JavaScript. A mean-only model is the required baseline; neighborhood collaborative filtering is a possible alternative, while a feature-based two-tower model could address additional information and cold starts, but neither alternative was benchmarked in this homework.

3. Method

3.1 Data and split

The supplied u.data contains 100,000 ratings from 943 users on 1,682 movies; all 1,682 movies have metadata. There are no duplicate user–movie pairs or missing-metadata ratings. Sparsity over real IDs is 1 − 100000 / (943 × 1682) = 93.69533064%; unused ID-zero padding is excluded. The pipe-separated u.item has 19 genre flags: “unknown” plus 18 named genres. Latin-1-compatible decoding preserves all nine accented titles.

For each user, sort by timestamp and then movie ID. Allocate floor(0.8n) training rows, floor(0.1n) validation rows, and the remainder to test. No user is skipped by the fewer-than-five-ratings rule. Equal timestamps cross train/validation boundaries for 470 users and validation/test boundaries for 472; the ordering is nondecreasing, not strictly separated in time.

3. Method (continued)

3.2 Model, exact objective, and checkpoint

The fixed training mean is μ = 285084 / 79619 = 3.5806026199776437. Raw predictions are μ + puTqi, with 16 factors. For each observed training mini-batch B:

LB = (1 / |B|) Σ(u,i,r)∈B (μ + puTqi − r)2 + λ (Σ Puf2 + Σ Qif2).

The data term is batch-mean MSE; L2 is the whole-matrix sum, including allocated/padding rows, applied on every mini-batch without division by batch size or factor count. This preserves the explicit upstream objective. Adam uses learning rate 0.01, batch size 64, and 20 epochs. Normal initialization has mean 0, SD 0.05, and seeds 5101/5102; Fisher–Yates shuffling uses Mulberry32 seed 5103. The only changed training parameter in the second run is λ: 0.05 → 0.005.

Tensor gathers and batched operations train only on observed training ratings; missing entries and held-out labels never enter gradient updates. Each strict validation-RMSE improvement creates independent factor snapshots; ties retain the earliest epoch. The selected factors are restored after training. Temporary tensors, superseded snapshots, optimizer state, and replaced models are disposed.

3.3 Rating and retrieval evaluation

For RMSE, clip predictions to [1,5] and use only users/items observed in training, with identical rows for baseline and MF. An allocated but untrained row is not a learned factor: scalar cold-start predictions fall back to μ and are excluded from reported warm-start RMSE; unknown users receive no recommendations.

Rank all training-observed movies with metadata after excluding the user's training/validation history. Ranking uses raw scores, with ascending movie ID for exact ties. Relevant test items have rating ≥4 and must be eligible candidates. Macro Recall@10 is the arithmetic mean of hits / eligible relevant items over users with a nonempty relevant set. Users 1 and 2 were fixed before the first model run and retained for the comparison.

4.3 Correctness and independent checks

The implementation passed 25 pretraining checks: nine upstream checks with unchanged assertions, two transparently corrected fixtures, and fourteen focused checks. The original recommendation fixture required an item absent from global training; it was corrected by adding another user's observation. The rank-11 fixture originally had only two eligible candidates; additional training-observed items made rank 11 possible. Their original test intent was preserved. Fixture tensor leaks and the incorrect disposal of optimizer.minimize(..., false, ...)'s null return were corrected.

Focused synthetic checks verified analytical prediction/clipping/loss, factor updates and loss reduction, exclusion of held-out labels from gradient updates, independent checkpoint copies and restoration, macro recall with unequal denominators, cold starts, metadata decoding, and repeated disposal. A deliberately conflicting synthetic validation set selected epoch 1 after eight epochs; restored factors matched an independent one-epoch run within 10−7. This was a constructed test, not a claimed MovieLens debugging incident.

Separate Python scripts loaded exact saved float32 factors and independently rebuilt splits/candidates. The default and follow-up passed 1,796 and 1,805 assertions, respectively; seventeen comparison checks confirmed the controlled setup. RMSE/score tolerance was 2×10−6; baseline/macro tolerance was 10−12. Both Top-10 orders matched exactly. Browser display, downloaded JSON, metadata and histories were cross-checked. All factors and history metrics were finite; two retained model tensors (168,128 bytes) returned to zero live tensors after disposal.

Initial data MSE is 1.4167258739 in both runs; scaled L2 falls from 5.2551331520 to 0.5255132914. Initial penalty/data-loss ratios are 3.70935 and 0.370935. Weaker regularization left larger residual factors, but corrections remained too small to affect μ. Thus the hypothesis of useful score variation and improved validation RMSE was not supported. The factors are not literally all zero; the model is effectively mean-only. Loss-value ratios are not gradient ratios and do not prove gradient dominance. This is one controlled seed/configuration comparison, not a universal optimum claim. Four WebGL ReadPixels warnings per run were saved; neither run failed. No further parameter search was performed.

5.2 Familiar Choices vs. New Discoveries

User 1's 244 historical ratings include 144 positives: Drama 68, Comedy 42 and Action 37 positive genre memberships. Fargo and Bound resemble the Crime/Thriller categories of five-star The Usual Suspects and Dolores Claiborne. Crumb is genre-familiar: all four historical documentaries were liked, including The Haunted World of Edward D. Wood Jr. Conversely, The Aristocats is less aligned with the historical Children's-category mean of 2.2083.

5. Discussion (continued)

User 2's 55 historical ratings include 38 positives: Drama 22, Romance 13 and Comedy 11. Crime/Thriller recommendations such as Copycat, Seven and The Usual Suspects resemble categories of five-star Fargo and L.A. Confidential. However, the list omits Romance despite positive ratings for Emma, Sense and Sensibility and Titanic. Babe represents a less frequent category, but Children's is already present in the history.

Neither list introduces a genre absent from its user's rated history. Familiar genre overlap might make recommendations plausible, but it is descriptive, not an explanation of learned ranking: all scores tie. An item unrated in the observed training/validation history is not necessarily unknown or unwatched. MovieLens does not provide complete exposure histories. Neither offline recall nor these examples establishes increased retention, satisfaction, or genuine discovery.

What worked: explicit eligibility, restored checkpoints, reproducible artifacts, and independent checks made the result auditable. What did not work: the specified objective/settings did not produce useful learned score variation, and a tenfold reduction of λ did not improve validation. Next step: review the intended scaling of batch-mean data loss against whole-matrix summed L2 with the instructor before authorizing a different objective or more experiments. This recommendation follows validation and factor diagnostics, not test-based parameter selection.
